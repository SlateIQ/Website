Definition

Cash is Lineup 1 in the Slate-IQ four-lineup structure — the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Cash games — 50/50s and double-ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same — reward a completely different mindset than tournaments. In a GPP (guaranteed prize pool, the big-field tournaments), you're chasing a ceiling and correlation is your friend. In cash, correlation doesn't help you; a high floor does. You don't need your quarterback and receiver to go off together — you need nine players who are individually unlikely to bust.

This week's refreshed build leans into that harder than the version it replaces: six of nine roster spots now carry the model's top CORE signal, up from five last time. Every player here needs to be a locked-in piece of his own offense, not competing with a teammate for touches or sitting behind a committee that could take his volume away.

What the Numbers Tell Us

Six of nine roster spots — Hurts, Taylor, Irving, Olave, London, and Wilson — carry the model's top CORE signal, the highest CORE count of any Cash build this season so far and a clear upgrade over the previous version of this lineup, which carried five. That's exactly the kind of foundation a cash lineup wants: minimizing bust risk across as much of the roster as possible.

Only one spot sits in WATCH territory — the Bears defense, priced just $200 over the position's VALUE cutoff. That's not a red flag on its own; Chicago's own implied total (25.0) is solid, it's simply a spot without the model's full endorsement.

Worth a brief mention: Irving and Otton both come from the same TB @ CIN game, the highest-total matchup on the slate (50.5). That's incidental rather than a deliberate correlation play — cash doesn't reward stacking the way GPPs do — but it's two of nine spots leaning on one game playing out well.

DFS Translation

How to read Grade and Signal: Grade buckets a player's raw point projection into A/B/C tiers using position-specific point thresholds — it's salary-blind, purely “how many points is this player expected to score.” Signal layers Value (projected points per $1,000 of salary) on top of Grade to flag roster fit:

  • CORE — Grade A and Value at or above a position-specific bar. Build your lineup around this player.
  • VALUE — Salary at or below a position-specific ceiling and Value at or above a bar. A cheap, efficient pivot that frees up cap space.
  • WATCH — Neither bar was cleared. Not a red flag, just no strong edge either way.

Final Takeaway

The Bears defense is the one true watch-list spot, and even that comes with a favorable game script behind it. Numbers don't lie, and this week they're telling a pretty clean story.

DFS Translation

How to read Grade and Signal: Grade buckets a player's raw point projection into A/B/C tiers using position-specific point thresholds — it's salary-blind, purely “how many points is this player expected to score.” Signal layers Value (projected points per $1,000 of salary) on top of Grade to flag roster fit:

  • CORE — Grade A and Value at or above a position-specific bar. Build your lineup around this player.
  • VALUE — Salary at or below a position-specific ceiling and Value at or above a bar. A cheap, efficient pivot that frees up cap space.
  • WATCH — Neither bar was cleared. Not a red flag, just no strong edge either way.

The exact cutoffs differ by position — a WR needs fewer raw points than a QB to earn a Grade A, for example:
