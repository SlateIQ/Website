Definition

The Cash Lineup is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

One housekeeping note before the roster: this week's real contest allocation ran 6 cash-style entries (3 Double-Ups, 3 50-50s) rather than the usual 4, and the Single-Entry/Three-Entry-Max GPP split didn't show up the way it normally does either — see the GPP posts for how that got handled. The Cash build itself is unaffected; it's still the same one roster entered across every cash-style contest this week.

The one real talking point in this build is the QB seat. Patrick Mahomes is the model's actual #1 quarterback on the slate by both raw projection and value, and Dak Prescott — the pick here — grades out a clear step behind him at #2 projection / #3 value. Dak is still a clean Grade-A, Signal-CORE play in his own right (Dallas is a 31-point implied team total, the highest on the board), so this isn't a bad lineup by any means — it's just worth knowing the single highest-conviction raw-points play on the slate isn't the one sitting in the Cash build this week.

Everywhere else, this is textbook Cash: cheap, efficient pieces at WR, the clear top tight end on the slate, and no reach anywhere on the roster.

What the Numbers Tell Us

Three of the nine roster spots (Burden, Odunze, and the Buccaneers/Irving pairing via their respective games) sit inside the slate's two highest-total environments — the CHI/NO 49-point game and the TB/DET 45.5-point game — without the roster ever actually stacking either one. That's the Cash build working as intended: high team totals are being used as a floor signal, not a correlation play. The one outlier is Dak Prescott sitting in the week's single highest implied-total environment (DAL at 31.0) while still grading behind Mahomes on raw points — a reminder that Cash Score blends Projection with Floor, so a QB in an uncontested, run-it-out-the-clock blowout spot can price in lower than a QB in a shootout even at a higher team total.

The Slate-IQ Take

This is a legitimately strong cash roster — eight of nine players are Grade A, and the one genuine swing-for-the-fences spot (Likely at TE) happens to also be the single best play at the position on the whole slate. The only honest hedge is the QB seat: Dak is a fine CORE play, but he is not the model's top QB this week, and that gap (26.6 points / 3.97 value vs. Mahomes' 28.1 / 4.33) is worth knowing going in, even though it doesn't change that this is still a high-floor, defensible build.

Final Takeaway

The highest-floor build of the week, picked purely on Projection and Floor with no correlation risk anywhere in the roster.

Definition

The Balanced Lineup is the hybrid build, still projection-driven but with some correlation and a few lower-owned pivots. It gets 3 of the week's 15 $1 entries, going into Single-Entry-Only GPPs, a format built for exactly one well-considered lineup rather than volume. Construction-wise it stays close to Cash: the same primary QB (or the secondary one), with just 1–2 player swaps for higher-upside, lower-owned pieces — moderate leverage and some correlation, not a full stack.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Same housekeeping note as the Cash post: this week's real contest allocation didn't include any Three-Entry-Max GPPs at all — Sam entered 9 "NFL $1 Hundo [Single Entry]" slots instead of the usual Single-Entry (3) plus Three-Entry-Max (8) split, so Balanced, Leverage, and Ceiling are sharing those 9 single-entry slots 3-3-3 this week rather than going to their normal destinations. The lineups themselves are unaffected; only where they're entered changed.

Construction-wise, this is a genuine double-stack, which is a step beyond Balanced's usual "no real same-game stack" description. Lawrence pivots off Cash's Dak Prescott at QB, and rather than treating Washington as a single isolated correlation piece, the build also rosters Brenton Strange at FLEX — both Jacksonville pass-catchers alongside Lawrence — with Isaiah Likely (New York Giants, JAX's opponent) serving as the mandatory bring-back. That's a real QB+2+1 shape, not the token single-piece correlation Balanced sometimes carries. The rest of the roster (both RBs, both remaining WRs) is held over directly from Cash, which keeps this build's overall risk profile close to the cash game despite the real stack sitting inside it.

Everything else in this build is either a Cash holdover or a Grade-A stack piece, so the downside case is fairly contained even if Strange busts.

Final Takeaway

The closest-to-Cash build of the three GPP lineups, but it still carries a real, mandatory QB+2+1 stack this week.
