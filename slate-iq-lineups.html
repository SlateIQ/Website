Definition

Cash is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier: one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This week's Cash build spends the full $50,000 — not a dollar left on the table — and still lands five Signal CORE plays: Bryce Young, Kenneth Walker III, Chris Olave, Isaiah Likely, and the Steelers defense. That's the highest conviction the model can offer, and none of it depends on any one game going a particular way. Every rostered player comes from a different game — all nine roster spots are drawn from nine separate matchups — so a single blowout, a single shootout, or a single early exit doesn't sink the whole roster.

Nobody on this roster carries an injury flag this week, which matters more for Cash than any other build, since Cash has no bench of correlated upside to fall back on if a piece goes down — it's nine independent bets on the model's best price-adjusted plays, full stop.

GPP #2 · Tournament Build

Week 3 GPP Lineup: Fading the Lions Blowout for a Ravens-Cowboys Shootout

This week's Ceiling build is a deliberate pivot: instead of the model's default Lions-anchored bring-back, it's built around the Ravens-Cowboys game — the slate's highest total and tightest spread — on the read that Jets-at-Lions is more likely a lopsided game than a shootout.

5-ManSame-Game Stack

Definition

Ceiling is the highest-ceiling, most aggressive-correlation build, willing to sacrifice floor for upside. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, paired one-for-one with a Leverage entry into Three-Entry Max GPPs. Construction-wise it leans hardest into game stacks and volatility — more volatile WRs, lower-owned players, and often a different QB and game environment entirely from the other three builds.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This build is a manual pivot off the model's default output, and it's worth explaining plainly. Left alone, the optimizer's Ceiling logic anchors on Vegas totals and typically lands on whichever game has the biggest number — this week, that was the Jets-at-Lions matchup (48-point total). The problem: Detroit is favored by 6.5 in that game, and the read here is that a game with that kind of spread is more likely to turn into a Lions blowout than an even shootout — good for Detroit's pass-catchers, bad for anyone hoping the Jets stay involved deep into the fourth quarter. The Ravens-at-Cowboys game, by contrast, carries the single highest total on the entire slate (52.5) with a spread of just 3 points — the tightest close-total game on the board, and the profile Slate-IQ wants for a correlated Ceiling build: a game Vegas expects to be both high-scoring and genuinely competitive on both sides.

So this build was re-solved with a hard requirement that the QB come from Baltimore or Dallas, and a minimum of five roster spots drawn from that game. DFS Translation

How to read Grade and Signal: Signal layers Value (projected points per $1,000 of salary) on top of Grade to flag roster fit:

  • CORE — Grade A and Value at or above a position-specific bar. Build your lineup around this player.
  • VALUE — Salary at or below a position-specific ceiling and Value at or above a bar. A cheap, efficient pivot that frees up cap space.
  • WATCH — Neither bar was cleared. Not a red flag, just no strong edge either way.

The exact cutoffs differ by position — a WR needs fewer raw points than a QB to earn a Grade A, for example:
