Definition

Cash is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier: one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Cash leans on the week's two highest-total NFC-adjacent games — SF@DEN and LAR@PHI, both inside 3.5 points — for four of its nine pieces (Purdy, Williams, Adams, and part of Watson's game slate), plus Travis Kelce as the week's clear highest-floor TE. There's no single roster-defining injury situation to account for this week: every player here is fully active. The one swap worth noting from a pure-floor standpoint is Parker Washington at WR3 — he's Grade A but sits on a WATCH signal, meaning his raw projection cleared the bar but his price tag didn't leave much room for a Value argument; he's here because JAX@CIN (51.5 total, the highest on the slate) gives him more raw ceiling than a cheaper, lower-total alternative, even in a lineup that otherwise avoids chasing upside.

5-ManJAX@CIN Stack

Definition

Balanced is the hybrid build, still projection-driven but with some correlation and a few lower-owned pivots. It gets 3 of the week's entries, going into Single-Entry-Only GPPs, a format built for exactly one well-considered lineup rather than volume. Construction-wise it stays close to Cash: the same primary QB (or the secondary one), with just 1–2 player swaps for higher-upside, lower-owned pieces — moderate leverage and some correlation, not a full stack.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Flagging this upfront: this week's Balanced build doesn't fit its usual mold. It rosters Trevor Lawrence, Parker Washington, and the Jaguars DST — all three from the JAX side of the week's highest-total game (JAX@CIN, 51.5) — plus Tee Higgins from the Cincinnati side. That's a genuine four-piece game stack, not the "no real stack, stands apart from the other GPP builds" construction Balanced normally gets. It still differs meaningfully from Leverage and Ceiling (different QB, different primary game environment), so it's doing its job as the third distinct GPP build — it's just doing it through correlation this week rather than through standing alone. Worth keeping an eye on: this means Balanced and the other GPP lineups aren't as independently diversified against a bad JAX@CIN game as the archetype definitions would normally promise.

Definition

Leverage is built around ownership discrepancies, where the field is likely wrong. It gets 4 of the week's entries, paired one-for-one with a Ceiling entry into Three-Entry Max GPPs — the stack-focused tournament format built around high-upside game correlation. Construction-wise this is 2–4 player changes from Cash, stronger game correlation than Balanced, and more ownership differentiation — leverage-driven substitutions rather than a straight upgrade.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This build leans harder into correlation than Leverage typically does. Patrick Mahomes, Travis Kelce, and Kenneth Walker III are all Kansas City pieces (the KC@LV game, 47.5 total), and Tre Tucker brings back the Las Vegas side of that same game — a full four-piece stack-and-bring-back on one matchup. On top of that, D'Andre Swift (CHI) and Garrett Wilson (NYJ) give this roster a second, separate correlation play in the CHI@NYJ game (43.5 total) — two players on opposite sides of a different matchup entirely. That's five of nine roster spots tied into just two games, which is a materially more concentrated build than a typical Leverage lineup, and it's worth knowing going in: this lineup's outcome depends heavily on two specific games breaking right, not nine independent plays.
