Definition

The Cash Lineup is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This week's cash build is unusual for carrying two Questionable tags on its two most expensive non-DST pieces. Christian McCaffrey (RB1, $8,000) and Malik Nabers (WR1, $7,300) both grade A/CORE and rank #1–2 in value at their positions — the model still considers them its best plays at RB and WR by a clear margin — but both carry a "Q" status heading into Sunday. That's a real risk concentration worth watching closely before this lineup actually gets entered into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where a single bust can flip a cashing lineup into a losing one.

Everywhere else, this is textbook Cash construction: Omar Cooper Jr. ($4,900) is the single best value play at any position on this roster (5.18 pts/$1K), Jameson Williams and Josh Jacobs are high-floor plays in good matchups, and Greg Dulcich at FLEX-TE ($3,100) is pure salary relief. The build leaves $1,400 of the $50,000 cap on the table, which is normal for the most efficient roster — Cash isn't chasing a max-projection combination, it's taking the cleanest play at each slot.

What the Numbers Tell Us

McCaffrey (30.25) and Jameson Williams (28.5) sit in the two highest implied-team-total games on this roster, while Cooper Jr. sits in the lowest (18.5, a heavy underdog) and still grades A on pure efficiency — the model isn't simply chasing Vegas favorites, it's finding value wherever the price is right. The more notable pattern this week: two of the roster's top four most expensive pieces (McCaffrey, Nabers) carry a Questionable tag simultaneously. That's an unusual amount of game-status risk concentrated at the top of a cash build, and it's worth monitoring through Sunday rather than assuming both play at full health.

The Slate-IQ Take

This is the most efficient roster the math can build this week, full stop — but it's leaning on two Questionable tags at its most expensive, highest-leverage positions. If either McCaffrey or Nabers gets downgraded to Doubtful or Out before lock, this build needs a second look; that's exactly the kind of swing a cash lineup can't absorb. Fannin at TE is the one WATCH-tier spot on an otherwise all-A-grade roster — not a concern on its own, just the lineup's lowest-conviction piece.

Final Takeaway

When floor is the only goal, the Cash Lineup takes the highest-probability plays on the board and lets the math do the rest — this week that math is sound, but it's carrying more injury-status risk at the top than usual, and that's worth a final check before kickoff.

Definition

It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, paired one-for-one with a Leverage entry into Three-Entry Max GPPs. Construction-wise it leans hardest into game stacks and volatility — more volatile WRs, lower-owned players, and often a different QB and game environment entirely from the other three builds.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This build runs two separate game stacks at once. The first is a four-player Miami/Detroit correlation — Malik Willis (QB), Malik Washington, and Greg Dulcich all on Miami, with Jahmyr Gibbs as the mandatory Detroit-side bring-back — which hits this build's per-game concentration cap exactly. The second is a smaller, three-player Las Vegas/San Francisco correlation: Christian McCaffrey on the 49ers' side against Tre Tucker and Michael Mayer on the Raiders' side, a 2-vs-1 bring-back arrangement rather than a same-team stack.
