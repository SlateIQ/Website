Definition

The Cash Lineup is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Cash doesn't need correlation to work this week — it has five A-grade CORE players (Maye, Warren, Williams, Nacua, and McMillan) plus Isaiah Likely as a sixth CORE anchor at a bargain TE salary, alongside two efficient B-grade VALUE pieces. The one real swing factor is injury risk: both Isaiah Likely (TE) and Keenan Allen (WR3) carry a Questionable tag into Sunday. Likely is a Grade-A CORE anchor, not a replaceable piece, so his snap count is worth checking one more time before lock. Neither tag is disqualifying on its own — Questionable players suit up more often than not — but a lineup built for a 50/50 can't absorb a surprise scratch at a CORE slot as easily as a GPP build can.

Definition

The Balanced Lineup is the hybrid build, still projection-driven but with some correlation and a few lower-owned pivots. It gets 3 of the week's 15 $1 entries, going into Single-Entry-Only GPPs, a format built for exactly one well-considered lineup rather than volume. Construction-wise it stays close to Cash: the same primary QB (or the secondary one), with just 1–2 player swaps for higher-upside, lower-owned pieces — moderate leverage and some correlation, not a full stack.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Flagging this up front because it doesn't match the usual pattern: this week's Balanced build is not a standalone, no-stack roster. Kirk Cousins (LV QB) and Michael Mayer (LV TE) are both rostered, and Keon Coleman (BUF WR) is the opponent bring-back for that same LV@BUF game — a legitimate three-player single-game correlation play, the kind of thing Balanced is normally built to avoid. On top of that, Bijan Robinson (ATL RB), Drake London (ATL WR), and the Falcons DST form a second, separate three-player cluster tied to the ATL@CHI game, even though none of them stack with the QB. Put together, 6 of this lineup's 9 roster spots are tied to just two games. Both clusters individually clear the Week 6 max-4-players-per-game cap (3 each), so nothing here is a rules violation — but it's a materially different risk profile from a typical Balanced build, and worth treating this week's Balanced more like a mini-Leverage build than the usual standalone one.

There's also a real, non-injury risk worth naming: Kirk Cousins' hold on the Raiders' starting job has drawn some media chatter after rookie Fernando Mendoza saw late-game mop-up snaps in Week 5's blowout loss. To be precise about it, Raiders coach Klint Kubiak has been direct that Cousins remains the Week 6 starter — there's no coach-level signal of an imminent change, just the kind of "is it time" speculation that follows any struggling veteran. Nothing here shows up as an Injury tag in the model; it's a storyline worth knowing about, not a real swing factor for this build's QB spot.

Construction-wise it leans hardest into game stacks and volatility — more volatile WRs, lower-owned players, and often a different QB and game environment entirely from the other three builds.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Flagging this up front, the way the Balanced write-up flagged its own construction surprise this week: this build's game stack is bigger than usual. Jameis Winston, Cam Skattebo, and Malik Nabers form a genuine 3-player same-team stack, and Devaughn Vele plus the Saints DST are the mandatory opponent bring-back from the other side of that same game. That's 5 of this lineup's 9 roster spots tied to one single game — NYG @ NO — which clears the Week 6 max-4-players-per-game cap built earlier this season specifically to guard against a Ceiling build leaning on too few games. Per the call to keep it as-built and flag it in the write-up rather than swap a piece, this stands as-is: the honest way to read it is that the mandatory-bring-back rule that defines Ceiling, taken to its logical extreme, produced a near-maximum single-game concentration this week. If NYG@NO goes sideways — a weather call, an early blowout that shuts down the losing offense's passing volume, a defensive-slot injury — it doesn't cost you one slot, it costs you more than half the roster at once.

It's also worth being direct about what this game actually is: NYG@NO projects to a 45.5-point total, which is middling for this slate, not the top game. The two highest-total games — LV@BUF at 53.5 and LAR@ARI at 51.5 — each get exactly one piece in this lineup (Brock Bowers and Davante Adams, respectively), rostered as standalone, uncorrelated plays rather than stacked into. So this build's upside case isn't "attach to the best game environment on the board" — it's "attach hard to a specific, moderate-total game and bet the correlation pays off bigger than chasing a marginally higher total would have."

What the Numbers Tell Us

The headline number is 130.37 — meaningfully below Cash (188.97), Balanced (166.85), and Leverage (183.39). That's not a mistake; it's the Ceiling archetype working as intended. This lineup wasn't selected to maximize the Blended Projection shown above — it was built off each player's Ceiling-column score (a separate, upside-only metric the model tracks), and on that basis this exact roster totals roughly 183 points, right in line with the other three builds. The gap between 130 and 183 for the same nine players is the entire point of a Ceiling build: it's betting on the spread between median and upper-bound outcomes, not on the median itself.

Construction-wise, two things stand out. First, the NYG@NO game stack: 5 of 9 slots (Winston, Skattebo, Nabers on the NYG side; Vele and the Saints DST on the NO side) all live or die together, which is both the deliberate mechanism behind this archetype's upside case and a genuine concentration risk. Second, the three standalone pieces outside that stack (Taylor, Adams, Bowers) aren't connected to each other or to the stack at all — they're simply the best individual Grade/projection plays available at their price points, landing in three different, unconnected game environments. Jonathan Taylor is the one true soft spot in that group: at $8,000 he's the most expensive player in the lineup, but his WATCH tag means the model doesn't see a clear edge in either direction at that price.

DFS Translation

How to read Grade and Signal: Signal layers Value (projected points per $1,000 of salary) on top of Grade to flag roster fit. The model now uses six Signal tags, not three:

  • CORE — Grade A and Value at or above a position-specific bar. Build your lineup around this player.
  • VALUE — Salary at or below a position-specific ceiling and Value at or above a bar. A cheap, efficient pivot that frees up cap space.
  • CEILING — Grade A or B and a Game Total (or, for DST, Favorite) percentile-rank at or above 70 — a signal this player's game environment supports upside, independent of Value.
  • LEVERAGE — Grade B or C, Value percentile-rank at or above 55, and projected-ownership percentile-rank at or below 35 — cheap production the field is likely under-rostering.
  • FADE — Grade C and projected-ownership percentile-rank at or above 65 — likely overpriced relative to expected ownership.
  • WATCH — Neither bar was cleared. Not a red flag, just no strong edge either way.

The exact cutoffs differ by position — a WR needs fewer raw points than a QB to earn a Grade A, for example:

The Slate-IQ Take

This is Ceiling doing exactly what it's built to do — maximum correlation, willing floor sacrifice, a real two-sided game stack instead of a one-sided one. The one thing worth sitting with before you fire this into a Three-Entry Max GPP: the stack grew from what was presumably a smaller concentration into a 5-player one once Skattebo and the Saints DST/Vele bring-back were layered on top of the existing Winston/Nabers pairing. That's not a construction error — it's the mandatory-bring-back rule taken to its natural extreme — but it does mean this lineup's single biggest swing factor isn't any one player's health or role, it's whether the NYG@NO game script cooperates at all. If it doesn't, more than half this roster is dead at once. Taylor's $8,000 WATCH tag is the other soft spot — not dangerous, just not earning its price tag the way the rest of the lineup's CORE pieces do.

Final Takeaway

This is Ceiling behaving exactly as designed — maximum correlation, lowest floor — but the stack grew one player too large for Week 6's own concentration rule, and that's a conscious call, not an oversight.
