Definition

The Cash Lineup is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Seven of the nine roster spots here are exactly what the Cash archetype promises: Jonathan Taylor, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, George Kittle, and the Broncos DST are all Grade A, and RJ Harvey and Michael Mayer are the single best-value players at their positions in the whole pool this week. That's a legitimately strong floor base.

The two spots that don't fit the mold as cleanly are QB and WR2. Jameis Winston grades out as a C with a WATCH signal — a 9.9-point projection at quarterback is not a floor play, it's closer to a dart throw, and it sits in the bottom third of the position both on raw projection and on value. Malik Nabers carries a Questionable tag on top of a middling value ranking (29th of 55 active WRs). Neither is disqualifying on its own, but together they're the two weakest links in what's supposed to be the safest lineup of the week — worth knowing going into 50/50s and Double-Ups specifically because that's a format where one dead roster spot can be the difference between cashing and not.

Definition

The Balanced Lineup is the hybrid build, still projection-driven but with some correlation and a few lower-owned pivots. It gets 3 of the week's 15 $1 entries, going into Single-Entry-Only GPPs, a format built for exactly one well-considered lineup rather than volume. Construction-wise it stays close to Cash: the same primary QB (or the secondary one), with just 1–2 player swaps for higher-upside, lower-owned pieces — moderate leverage and some correlation, not a full stack.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Flagging this upfront: Nico Collins is in this lineup's WR2 spot, and he is Out this week. That's not a leverage call or a risk to weigh — a rostered player who doesn't play scores zero, full stop, in a Single-Entry GPP format where there's no second chance to fix it mid-slate. This needs to be addressed before this lineup goes anywhere near a real entry.

Setting that spot aside, the rest of the construction fits the Balanced archetype well — Brock Purdy at QB instead of Cash's Jameis Winston is exactly the kind of "secondary primary QB" swap the archetype calls for, and Luther Burden III and Matthew Golden are the lower-salary, higher-signal pivots that give this build its edge over a straight Cash clone. The FLEX spot is a second soft point worth flagging on its own: Isaiah Likely carries a Questionable tag and the weakest Grade/Signal combination on the roster (Grade C, WATCH) even without the injury concern layered on top.

Definition

DFS Translation

How to read Grade and Signal: Signal layers Value (projected points per $1,000 of salary) on top of Grade to flag roster fit:

  • CORE — Grade A and Value at or above a position-specific bar. Build your lineup around this player.
  • VALUE — Salary at or below a position-specific ceiling and Value in the top third of the active pool at that position. A cheap, efficient pivot that frees up cap space.
  • WATCH — Neither bar was cleared. Not a red flag, just no strong edge either way.

The exact cutoffs differ by position — a WR needs fewer raw points than a QB to earn a Grade A, for example:

Ashton Jeanty's Questionable tag is the one flag worth a last look before lock; everything else here is a known, deliberate trade-off rather than a surprise.
