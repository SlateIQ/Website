Definition

The Cash Lineup is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Week 8's Cash build is about as clean as this model produces: Drake Maye is the single best value play at any position on the entire slate (5.01, well clear of the next-best QB), and seven of the other eight slots are Grade-A/CORE plays in their own right. There's no injury risk anywhere on this roster — every slot is fully active.

The one slot that isn't CORE is Hunter Henry at FLEX (Grade B, WATCH). That's not a red flag so much as math: once the eight CORE pieces above are locked in, Henry is simply the best remaining efficiency play left in the pool at that price point. No-stacking, no-ownership-consideration construction means this lineup is allowed to leave one slot at "good, not great" if that's genuinely the best points-per-dollar left on the board.

Maye projects for 32.6 points, 1st among all active QBs in both projection and value (1st/27 on both counts). NE is favored by 7.5 in a 44.5-point game, the kind of comfortable-favorite spread that still keeps a quarterback's volume up without the game getting out of hand. No injury concern.

RB1 — Jaylen Warren ($6,600, PIT vs. CLE)

RB2 — Bucky Irving ($6,400, TB vs. ATL)

WR1 — Parker Washington ($6,500, JAX vs. IND)

WR2 — Jameson Williams ($6,000, DET vs. MIN)

What the Numbers Tell Us

This build spans six different games (NE/MIA, PIT/CLE, TB/ATL, JAX/IND, DET/MIN, LV/NYJ), which is simply where the nine highest-efficiency plays happened to fall — Cash's construction rule doesn't diversify games on purpose, it just doesn't stack them either. The one coincidental concentration worth noting: three of the nine slots (Bucky Irving, Kyle Pitts Sr., Buccaneers DST) all come from the same TB @ ATL game. That's not a stack by this model's definition (no shared-team correlation logic was applied to pick them), but if that game script goes sideways — a blowout either direction — it touches a third of this lineup's slots at once.

The Slate-IQ Take

Eight of nine slots are Grade-A/CORE plays, and the one exception (Hunter Henry, WATCH) is a genuinely low-risk hedge rather than a real weak link — he's still a rostered, active TE at a fair price, just without the Grade-A ceiling the rest of the roster clears. The bigger thing to watch isn't any single player's grade, it's the incidental TB@ATL concentration described above — worth keeping an eye on Tampa Bay–Atlanta's actual game flow Sunday, even though nothing here was built around that game on purpose.

Final Takeaway

No stacks, no ownership reads, just the nine most efficient plays the model could find for $49,200 — this is the lineup every other build gets measured against this week.

Definition

The Balanced Lineup is the hybrid build, still projection-driven but with some correlation and a few lower-owned pivots. It gets 3 of the week's 15 $1 entries, going into Single-Entry-Only GPPs, a format built for exactly one well-considered lineup rather than volume. Construction-wise it stays close to Cash: the same primary QB (or the secondary one), with just 1–2 player swaps for higher-upside, lower-owned pieces — moderate leverage and some correlation, not a full stack.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

Week 8 is the first week Balanced actually has to carry a real stack — the Oct 2026 DFS Army rule update extended the QB + 2 pass-catchers + bring-back requirement to all three GPP builds, where previously Balanced got away with a single same-team piece and no bring-back at all. That shows up directly in this roster: Trevor Lawrence anchors a genuine Jacksonville stack (Parker Washington and Brenton Strange) with Keenan Allen pulled in specifically as the mandatory Indianapolis bring-back.

Keenan Allen is also this build's one injury flag — he's Questionable. He's filling a mandatory structural role (the bring-back), not a best-available-player pick, so there wasn't a same-price alternative without a tag; the risk is real but it's a known, accepted one rather than an oversight.
