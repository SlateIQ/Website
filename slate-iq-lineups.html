Why This Lineup, Why Now

Cash games — 50/50s and double-ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same — reward a completely different mindset than tournaments. In a GPP (guaranteed prize pool, the big-field tournaments), you're chasing a ceiling and correlation is your friend. In cash, correlation doesn't help you; a high floor does. You don't need your quarterback and receiver to go off together — you need nine players who are individually unlikely to bust.

That's the lens for this build: every player here needs to be a locked-in piece of his own offense, not competing with a teammate for touches or sitting behind a committee that could take his volume away.

When a model likes a player at a price this far off his salary tier for that reason, that's the headline, not a footnote.

The model's least confident grade in the lineup — a C, with a WATCH signal and an 8.52-point projection. He's here because tight end is thin league-wide and $3,900 is cheap enough to accept a modest floor rather than pay up at a position with little separation between options.

FLEX — Garrett Wilson ($5,800, NYJ @ TEN)
An A grade and CORE signal at 16.88 points — one of five CORE-tier plays in this lineup. New York's a 1.5-point favorite in a lower 38.5-point game (18.5 implied), the lowest team total rostered here, but the model still likes his role enough to grade him top-tier.

The Slate-IQ Take

This is a lineup with a real floor across most of the roster — Taylor, London, McMillan, and Wilson are all A-grade, CORE-signal plays, which is about as good as cash-game construction gets. Where I'd pump the brakes slightly is Henry: he's defensible on role and game environment, but I'm rostering him because of what I know about Baltimore's offense, not because the model is telling me he's clearly the right price at $6,700. Andrews is a different kind of compromise — a cheap floor play at a thin position, not a mistake, just not a strength.

Final Takeaway

Cash games aren't won with the sexiest picks — they're won by not giving points away. Five CORE-grade players and two VALUE plays give this lineup a real foundation; Henry is the one true watch-list spot worth a last look before kickoff, with Andrews' cheap floor at tight end being the other necessary trade-off, not a red flag.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

GPPs (guaranteed prize pool tournaments) reward completely different things than cash games. In a large-field GPP, you're not trying to beat half the field — you're trying to beat almost all of it. That means two things matter here that don't matter in cash: correlation (rostering players who score off the same plays) and leverage (exposure to players the field probably isn't rostering).

This build leans on both. Four players — Trevor Lawrence, Jaylen Warren, Jauan Jennings, and Dallas Goedert — are here because the model likes them (Grade B or better) without tagging them CORE, priced outside the top of their position. Layered on top: three of the other five roster spots (Bijan Robinson, Drake London, Kyle Pitts) come from the same game, Steelers-Falcons, giving this lineup real correlated upside if that game turns into a shootout.
