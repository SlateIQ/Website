Definition

The Cash Lineup is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This week's Cash build is about as clean as the model gets: every skill-position starter and the DST carries a Grade A, and seven of the nine roster spots land on CORE or CASH-CORE signal. C.J. Stroud anchors it as the single best raw-points play in the entire QB pool this week, and Tetairoa McMillan is the single best play at any position among wide receivers. Isaiah Likely rounds out the anchors at tight end — the clear top projection at the position (CORE signal, Grade A) and, per Friday's official injury report, carrying no designation at all: a true anchor with a clean bill of health, not a thin margin.

Because this build doesn't optimize for correlation at all, a few incidental game overlaps showed up anyway — Chuba Hubbard and Tetairoa McMillan are both Panthers, and Aaron Jones Sr./Keenan Allen and C.J. Stroud/Isaiah Likely are each opposite-side pairs from the same games. None of that was built in; it's just what happens when several of the week's best individual plays come from the same few high-total games.

QB — C.J. Stroud ($5,700, HOU vs. NYG)
Stroud projects for 24.67 points, the #1 raw projection at quarterback this week and the #1 value play at the position (4.33 pts/$1K). RB2 — Aaron Jones Sr. ($6,200, MIN vs. IND)
19.75 points projected, ranking 5th at the position but 2nd in value (3.19 pts/$1K). Minnesota's 41.5-point game with a 6.5-point edge in Jones's favor implies 24 points for his own offense — a strong floor environment for a lead-role back.

WR1 — Tetairoa McMillan ($7,100, CAR vs. TB)
31.4 points projected — the single highest raw projection AND the single highest value (4.42 pts/$1K) of any wide receiver on the slate this week. The clear anchor of this roster.

WR2 — Matthew Golden ($6,000, GB vs. DET)
19.47 points projected, 4th among receivers and 5th in value. Golden sits in the week's highest-total game (52.5), with Green Bay favored by 3 — the best team-implied total on the board (24.75) feeding his target share.

TE — Isaiah Likely ($5,300, NYG vs. HOU)
22.63 points projected, the #1 raw projection and #2 value tight end on the slate (4.27 pts/$1K). Likely carries no injury designation this week, confirmed directly against the Giants' official Week 7 report — the model's clear top play at the position, rostered here as a true CORE anchor.

FLEX — Sam LaPorta ($4,800, DET vs. GB)

DST — Jets ($2,900, NYJ vs. MIA)
10 points projected, 5th among defenses in both raw points and value (3.45 pts/$1K). A Grade A, cash-reliable defense in a game where the Jets are favored by 4.5 — exactly the low-variance DST profile Cash is built around.

What the Numbers Tell Us

DFS Translation

How to read Grade and Signal: Signal layers Value (projected points per $1,000 of salary) on top of Grade to flag roster fit. The model now uses six Signal tags, not three:

  • CORE — Grade A and Value at or above a position-specific bar. Build your lineup around this player.
  • VALUE — Salary at or below a position-specific ceiling and Value at or above a bar. A cheap, efficient pivot that frees up cap space.
  • CASH-CORE — Grade A with a slightly lower Value bar than CORE — built specifically for cash-game reliability.
  • CEILING — Grade A or B and a Game Total (or, for DST, Favorite) percentile-rank at or above 70 — a signal this player's game environment supports upside, independent of Value.
  • LEVERAGE — Grade B or C, Value percentile-rank at or above 55, and projected-ownership percentile-rank at or below 35 — cheap production the field is likely under-rostering.
  • WATCH — Neither bar was cleared. Not a red flag, just no strong edge either way.

Definition

The Balanced Lineup is the hybrid build, still projection-driven but with some correlation and a few lower-owned pivots. It gets 3 of the week's 15 $1 entries, going into Single-Entry-Only GPPs, a format built for exactly one well-considered lineup rather than volume. Construction-wise it stays close to Cash: the same primary QB (or the secondary one), with just 1–2 player swaps for higher-upside, lower-owned pieces — moderate leverage and some correlation, not a full stack.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

A flag before anything else: this week's Balanced build doesn't land cleanly in the "no real stack" pattern the archetype usually follows. Jared Goff (DET) and Sam LaPorta (DET) are the same team, and Matthew Golden (GB) is the opponent bring-back in that exact game — that's a genuine 3-player, single-game stack, not incidental overlap. On top of that, Christian McCaffrey (SF) and Kyle Pitts Sr. (ATL) are opposite sides of the same SF/ATL game. So rather than the lighter-correlation build the Balanced definition describes, this lineup actually carries real game-stack construction on two different games. It's being published as Balanced because it's going into the Single-Entry GPP slot and doesn't have the deliberate ownership-leverage framing of this week's Leverage build, but worth knowing going in: this one leans harder into correlation than "Balanced" usually means.

That correlation isn't an accident worth worrying about, though — it's anchored on good numbers. Detroit's game with Green Bay carries a 52.5 total, the single highest on this week's slate, with both Goff and Golden/LaPorta pulling from that environment. Pivoting the QB away from Cash's Stroud also differentiates this build's ownership profile from the Cash lineup without needing to force anything.

QB — Jared Goff ($6,800, DET vs. GB)
26.21 points projected, 2nd among all rostered-week quarterbacks but 4th in value (3.85 pts/$1K) given the higher salary. RB1 — Christian McCaffrey ($7,600, SF vs. ATL)
21.67 points projected, 4th at the position but only 5th in value given the top-of-market salary. San Francisco's 46.5-point game with a 1.5-point edge implies 24 points for McCaffrey's own offense — a solid, if not standout, environment for the slate's most expensive running back.

RB2 — Kyren Williams ($7,000, LAR vs. LV)
22 points projected, 3rd at the position and 3rd in value (3.14 pts/$1K). Williams sits in a 47.5-point game with the Rams favored by 5.5, implying 26.5 points for his offense — the best game environment of this lineup's two backs.

WR1 — Malik Nabers ($6,800, NYG vs. HOU)
29.93 points projected, 2nd at the position in both raw points and value (4.40 pts/$1K). A near-elite play at the position for a mid-tier salary.

WR2 — Matthew Golden ($6,000, GB vs. DET)
19.47 points projected, 4th at the position. This is the piece that completes this build's Detroit-game stack — Golden is the bring-back on the other side of Goff and LaPorta's game, in the slate's highest-total environment.

WR3 — Wan'Dale Robinson ($4,600, TEN vs. CLE)
9.17 points projected — the lowest raw projection of any rostered player in this portfolio, ranking 26th among receivers, though his low salary still clears the VALUE bar on Value percentile. This is the one real soft spot in the build: a true punt play, not a projection-driven pick, included purely to fit the Detroit stack and McCaffrey/Pitts pairing under the cap.

TE — Kyle Pitts Sr. ($4,000, ATL vs. SF)
15.3 points projected, 4th at tight end and 3rd in value (3.83 pts/$1K). Pitts is the bring-back piece on the other side of McCaffrey's SF/ATL game — a correlated pairing even though the two don't share a team.

FLEX — Sam LaPorta ($4,800, DET vs. GB)
21.1 points projected, 2nd at tight end in raw points and the #1 value tight end on the slate (4.40 pts/$1K). LaPorta is the second piece of this build's Detroit stack, pairing directly with Goff.

DST — Buccaneers ($2,200, TB vs. CAR)
8 points projected, 12th among defenses in raw points but 4th in value (3.64 pts/$1K) given the cheap salary. Tampa Bay is favored by 10 in a 41-point game — that lopsided spread is exactly the kind of environment that tends to produce extra defensive scoring chances.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This build goes further than the standard single-game anchor the Ceiling archetype usually describes — it stacks two different high-total games at once. The first is a 3-player Cincinnati/Baltimore stack (Joe Burrow + Zay Flowers + Mike Gesicki) built around the week's second-highest game total (51.5). The second is a 3-player Green Bay/Detroit stack (MarShawn Lloyd + Christian Watson + Jameson Williams) built around the week's single highest game total (52.5). That's six of nine roster spots committed to just two games going high-scoring — the clearest "sacrifice floor for upside" construction of the four lineups this week. The QB swap to Joe Burrow (from Stroud in Cash, Goff in Balanced, Young in Leverage) also gives this build a completely different game environment from the rest of the portfolio, as the archetype calls for.

The floor risk is concentrated in two spots: MarShawn Lloyd projects for just 9.2 points, the 18th-ranked running back on the slate, included purely to complete the GB/DET stack rather than on his own merits; and Mike Gesicki's 10.2-point floor at tight end is the thinnest of any rostered player in this portfolio. Both are acceptable in a Ceiling build whose job is to hit big, not to be safe.

What the Numbers Tell Us

This build posts the lowest total projection of the four lineups (147.48, versus 191.53 for Leverage and 187.37 for Cash) — a direct, visible consequence of trading floor for upside. Six of nine spots are tied to just two games (CIN/BAL and GB/DET), both among the slate's three highest totals, meaning this lineup's outcome depends heavily on two specific shootouts actually happening rather than on nine independent good games. The low-floor pieces (Lloyd, Gesicki) are concentrated exactly where the stacks needed cap relief.

DFS Translation

How to read Grade and Signal: Signal layers Value (projected points per $1,000 of salary) on top of Grade to flag roster fit. The model now uses six Signal tags, not three:

  • CORE — Grade A and Value at or above a position-specific bar. Build your lineup around this player.
  • VALUE — Salary at or below a position-specific ceiling and Value at or above a bar. A cheap, efficient pivot that frees up cap space.
  • CASH-CORE — Grade A with a slightly lower Value bar than CORE — built specifically for cash-game reliability.
  • CEILING — Grade A or B and a Game Total (or, for DST, Favorite) percentile-rank at or above 70 — a signal this player's game environment supports upside, independent of Value.
  • LEVERAGE — Grade B or C, Value percentile-rank at or above 55, and projected-ownership percentile-rank at or below 35 — cheap production the field is likely under-rostering.
  • WATCH — Neither bar was cleared. Not a red flag, just no strong edge either way.

The exact cutoffs differ by position — a WR needs fewer raw points than a QB to earn a Grade A, for example:

The Slate-IQ Take

This is the correct shape for a Ceiling build — real sacrifice of projection for correlation, and two legitimate high-total games doing the work rather than one. The honest hedge: MarShawn Lloyd's 9.2-point floor and Mike Gesicki's thin 7.14-point floor mean this lineup has real bust potential if either Green Bay/Detroit or Cincinnati/Baltimore turns into a low-scoring, run-heavy game instead of the shootout the Vegas totals suggest. That's the trade this archetype is built to make, but it's worth going in with eyes open about where the risk actually sits.

Final Takeaway

Built to win big on two specific shootouts, not to cash safely — the lowest floor in the portfolio is the price of the highest correlation.
