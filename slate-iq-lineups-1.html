Definition

The Cash Lineup is the highest-floor, most optimal build, and the one official cash lineup Slate-IQ enters every week. It gets 4 of the week's 15 $1 entries, all placed into 50/50s and Double-Ups, where roughly half the field cashes and everyone who does gets paid the same. The build skews heavily toward the model's highest-confidence tier (75–85% core): one primary QB, high-floor RBs and WRs, a safe TE, and a strong DST — no stacking, no ownership consideration, just the most efficient roster available. It's also the fixed benchmark every other lineup gets measured against each week.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This week's Cash Lineup spends all $50,000 and breaks one of the archetype's usual rules on purpose: it pairs Dak Prescott with CeeDee Lamb. Normally Cash takes no stack at all, but this week the pairing is the efficient play on its own merits. Dak is the #3 QB projection on the slate (21.53) at a mid-range $6,400, and Lamb is the #2 WR projection (24.90) in the slate's highest-total game (SF at DAL, 55.5). Taking both concentrates some risk in one offense, and that trade-off is accepted knowingly rather than by accident.

The rest is floor and price. Emmett Johnson moves into the starting job in Kansas City with Kenneth Walker ruled out, at a $4,700 price set before that news. Xavier Hutchinson (2.98 pts/$1K) and Darren Waller (3.44, the #2 value TE) are the salary relief that makes the Lamb–Olave–Chuba core affordable. The Seahawks D faces a rookie making his first NFL start.

QB — Dak Prescott ($6,400, DAL vs. SF)
21.53 projected points, #3 of 25 QBs in raw projection and #6 in value (3.36 pts/$1K). SF at DAL carries the slate's highest game total (55.5); Dallas is a 3.5-point underdog, for an implied team total of 26.0. A home underdog in a high-total game is the cleanest QB environment on the board, and he's paired with Lamb in this build.

RB1 — Chuba Hubbard ($7,200, CAR vs. NO)
16.17 projected points, #10 of 43 RBs and #14 in value. CAR at NO totals 48.5 with Carolina a 2.5-point underdog (implied 23.0). He handles nearly all of Carolina's backfield work against a defense that has been the softest against RBs, which is the definition of a high-floor cash RB.

RB2 — Emmett Johnson ($4,700, KC vs. ATL)
13.80 projected points, #13 of 43 RBs and #5 in value (2.94 pts/$1K). Kansas City is a 3.25-point favorite in a 45.75 total (implied 24.5). With Kenneth Walker ruled out, Johnson steps into the lead role at a backup's price. The model's projection was set before that news, so it reflects the price rather than the new workload.

WR1 — CeeDee Lamb ($8,800, DAL vs. SF)
24.90 projected points, #2 of 49 WRs and #6 in value. Same 55.5-total game as Dak, implied 26.0. The most expensive player on the roster and the single biggest projection, he's the anchor this build is organized around.

WR2 — Chris Olave ($7,000, NO vs. CAR)
14.20 projected points, #9 of 49 WRs. New Orleans is a 2.5-point favorite in the 48.5 game (implied 25.5). He's the clear first read in this offense, and that target volume is what makes him a floor play rather than a boom-or-bust one.

WR3 — Xavier Hutchinson ($4,000, HOU vs. CLE)
11.90 projected points, #12 of 49 WRs and #5 in value (2.98 pts/$1K). Houston is a 5-point favorite in a 40.25 total (implied 22.63). He runs most of his routes from the slot, away from Cleveland's best cover corner. He's the cheapest WR on the roster and the piece that funds Lamb.

TE — Darren Waller ($3,600, CAR vs. NO)
12.37 projected points, #6 of 26 TEs and #2 in value (3.44 pts/$1K). Same CAR at NO game as Chuba (implied 23.0). The best price-to-projection TE on the slate, coming off a 10-target game.

FLEX — Kaelon Black ($5,000, SF vs. DAL)
9.45 projected points, #23 of 43 RBs and #22 in value. San Francisco is a 3.5-point favorite in the 55.5 game (implied 29.5, the highest on this roster). Dallas sees more running plays against it than any defense on the slate, and he gives this lineup a cheap piece of the game's other side. He's the lowest-conviction projection in the build.

DST — Seahawks ($3,300, SEA vs. LV)
7.33 projected points, #9 of 20 DSTs. Seattle is a 6.5-point favorite in a 40.5 total, leaving Las Vegas an implied 17.0. The Raiders start a rookie making his first NFL start, the classic setup for sacks and turnovers.

What the Numbers Tell Us

The salary is concentrated where the projections are. Lamb, Dak, Chuba and Olave account for $29,400 of the cap and 76.8 of the 131.65 projected points. Three of the nine players come from SF at DAL, the slate's highest total, and three from CAR at NO, the next-closest-to-even game. That's two games carrying two-thirds of the roster, which is more concentration than a typical Cash build. The value end does the rest. Waller, Hutchinson and Johnson are three of the roster's four best values (3.44, 2.98 and 2.94 pts/$1K, with Dak at 3.36), and together they cost $12,300. Kaelon Black is the exception: the lowest projection and value here, kept for his role in the slate's best game rather than for his number.

The Slate-IQ Take

This is a high-floor build with two conscious departures from the textbook: the Dak–Lamb pairing and Kaelon Black at FLEX. The pairing is defensible because both rank near the top of their positions on raw projection. Black is the lineup's weakest link on paper. Johnson's role is the bigger swing factor: a starter priced as a backup is exactly the kind of edge cash lineups exist to take, but it also means the model hasn't caught up to his new workload yet. No rostered player carries an injury tag.

Final Takeaway

When floor is the only goal, the Cash Lineup takes the best price-to-projection plays on the board. This week that means leaning into the slate's highest-total game with Dak and Lamb and letting the value plays carry the rest.

Definition

The Balanced Lineup is the hybrid build, still projection-driven but with some correlation and a few lower-owned pivots. It gets 3 of the week's 15 $1 entries, going into Single-Entry-Only GPPs, a format built for exactly one well-considered lineup rather than volume. Construction-wise it stays close to Cash: the same primary QB (or the secondary one), with just 1–2 player swaps for higher-upside, lower-owned pieces — moderate leverage and some correlation, not a full stack.

Why This Lineup, Why Now

This week's Balanced build doesn't follow the usual template, and it's worth saying so plainly. Rather than keeping Cash's QB and making one or two swaps, it moves to a different QB (Tyler Shough) and carries a full Saints stack: Shough with Chris Olave and Jordyn Tyson, and Chuba Hubbard as the bring-back from Carolina. That's four changes from Cash (Shough, Tyson, AJ Barner, Vikings D), more than the archetype normally calls for.

The reason is the game. CAR at NO is a 48.5-total game with a 2.5-point spread, between two pass-leaning offenses and two defenses that have given up points all season. Shough at $5,600 is the #7 QB in value, which leaves room to keep Cash's best pieces (Lamb, Chuba, Olave, Johnson, Kaelon Black) around the stack. It stays closer to Cash than the other two GPP builds, which is the part of the archetype it does hold to.

QB — Tyler Shough ($5,600, NO vs. CAR)
17.26 projected points, #8 of 25 QBs and #7 in value (3.08 pts/$1K). New Orleans is a 2.5-point favorite in the 48.5 game, implied 25.5. He's at home with a thin Saints running game, which keeps the scoring in his hands.

RB1 — Chuba Hubbard ($7,200, CAR vs. NO)
16.17 projected points, #10 of 43 RBs. Carolina's implied total is 23.0. He's the bring-back from the other side of the Saints game, held over from Cash: if this game shoots out, he's likely part of why.

RB2 — Emmett Johnson ($4,700, KC vs. ATL)
13.80 projected points, #13 of 43 RBs and #5 in value. Kansas City implied 24.5. The new starter with Kenneth Walker ruled out, at a price set before that news. Held over from Cash, outside the stack.

WR1 — Chris Olave ($7,000, NO vs. CAR)
14.20 projected points, #9 of 49 WRs. Stack piece #1: Shough's first read, held over from Cash.

WR2 — Jordyn Tyson ($5,000, NO vs. CAR)
6.00 projected points, #36 of 49 WRs. Stack piece #2 and the lowest-projected player in the lineup. The model's number leans on very little history: he's only just back from injury, and in his first game back he moved straight into a starting role alongside Olave. He's here for his role in the stack, not his projection, and his FADE signal is the honest risk flag on this roster.

WR3 — CeeDee Lamb ($8,800, DAL vs. SF)

FLEX — Kaelon Black ($5,000, SF vs. DAL)

What the Numbers Tell Us

Four of the nine players come from CAR at NO, the maximum this portfolio allows from one game, and they're the correlated core: Shough, Olave and Tyson on one side, Chuba on the other. Lamb and Kaelon Black add two pieces of SF at DAL, so six of nine players are in the slate's two most attractive scoring environments. The projection is uneven by design. Lamb (24.90), Shough (17.26) and Chuba (16.17) carry most of it, while Tyson (6.00) is a stack piece the model doesn't believe in yet. The model's ownership projection has Shough at 4.6% and Tyson at 3.5%, so the stack itself shouldn't be crowded, even though Lamb (14.6%) and Chuba (12.9%) will be popular.

The Slate-IQ Take

This is the most Cash-like of the three GPP builds, but it isn't a light touch: a different QB, a real stack and four changes. The Saints game is the bet, and if it plays to its total this lineup has the core of it. The honest hedges are Tyson (a FADE signal on a thin projection), Barner (WATCH) and Kaelon Black (the lowest-value RB on the roster). Those three are where this build is buying role and price rather than projection. No rostered player carries an injury tag.

Final Takeaway

The Balanced Lineup stays close to Cash where it can and commits to one game where it can't: a Saints stack with Chuba coming back, plus Lamb as the one-off in the slate's highest-total game.

Definition

Why This Lineup, Why Now
